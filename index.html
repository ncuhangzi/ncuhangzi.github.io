01 / Profile

The
serving layer.

Getting a language model to answer once is easy. Keeping it answering for a whole company, at a latency people will tolerate and on hardware someone has to pay for, is a different job. That second one is mine.

I came at this from information management rather than systems: two degrees at National Central University, a master's thesis on multimodal recommendation, then a second master's in information systems at Northeastern University. Research taught me to read a model's numbers. Consulting taught me what those numbers cost in GPU hours.

At Taiwan Web Service I sized and deployed vLLM inference services, working out which model and which machine a client's workload actually needed. At Delta I do that for one large company instead of many: hardware sizing, inference architecture, routing across models, and enough tracing through Langfuse and OpenTelemetry to tell which model, prompt or tool call is doing the work.

One of the more interesting things I have deployed recently is gpt-oss-120b on a laptop: an HP ZBook Ultra G1a with an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ PRO 395. 120 billion parameters, no data centre.

The part I care about is whether you can see inside a running system. Without traces you are guessing, and with LLMs you are guessing about something that changes every time you swap a model.
